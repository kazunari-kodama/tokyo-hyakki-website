ABOUT
Koenji opens its doors to the other world once more.

Tokyo Hyakki Yagyo — "the Night Parade of One Hundred Demons" — is the largest yokai parade in the Kanto region, with yokai (Japanese spirits and monsters) gathering from all over Japan.

On this day the gate to the underworld opens in Koenji, and yokai roam the streets: relaxing in cafés, snacking on street food, eating set meals. Yokai everywhere you go!

It is held on the same day as the Koenji Festival, one of the neighborhood's biggest events, so the whole town is in a festive mood. The entire Koenji Look Shopping Street welcomes yokai, and you are welcome to chat with the shops and locals — and to watch the yokai give them a scare.

Manners
  • Please watch from the side of the street and keep the parade route clear.
  • Please do not touch the yokai or pull on their costumes.
  • When taking photos up close, please ask first.
  • Please do not stop to take photos in front of shops or anywhere you might get in the way of business.
  • Please follow the instructions of the guide staff (they wear armbands).
Photos & Social Media

Photography is allowed throughout the Koenji Festival area. When posting, please respect people's privacy. We'd love it if you used the hashtags #東京百鬼夜行 #高円寺ルック商店街 #高円寺フェス.

Good to Know
Traffic
The parade route is closed to traffic on the day. Please do not come by car; use public transportation.
Rain
The event goes ahead in light rain. In stormy weather the parade may be cancelled. Updates are posted on our official X account (@tokyo_hyakki).
Restrooms
Please use restaurants or public restrooms. The restrooms at the Look Shopping Street office and Wagakki BAR Ryugu are for yokai participants only.
Feeling unwell
If you feel unwell or get hurt, please tell a nearby guide staff member.

Main Rules
  • Costumes of copyrighted characters (anime, etc.) are not allowed, including arrangements that would generally be seen as such.
  • Items taller than 2 m or wider than 1 m are not allowed.
  • No excessive gore makeup or blood-splattered costumes. Fake blood, paint, color spray, nail polish and anything else that could stain the surroundings may not be used on the day.
  • No costumes that drag on the ground, and nothing with sharp parts that could hurt people or catch on things.
  • No overly revealing costumes.
  • No walking around while drinking alcohol, as it may spill on others.
  • Replica swords may be carried sheathed, but drawing them is not allowed anywhere.
  • Children of elementary school age and under must be accompanied by a guardian.
  • You may be photographed by many people and posted on social media without permission or editing.

FAQ
Frequently Asked Questions
Q. Does it cost anything to watch?
No. Watching is free, and you don't need to register or buy a ticket.
Q. Where is the best place to watch?
The departure ceremony at 14:00 in front of Queen's Isetan Shin-Koenji is a great chance for close-up photos. The finale at Koenji Station North Exit Plaza gets very crowded.
Q. What happens if it rains?
The event goes ahead in light rain. In stormy weather the parade may be cancelled. Updates are posted on our official X account (@tokyo_hyakki).
Q. Can I take photos?
Yes, anywhere in the Koenji Festival area. Please ask before taking close-up photos, and do not take photos in front of shops where you might get in the way of business.
Q. Where are the restrooms?
Please use restaurants or public restrooms. The restrooms at the Look Shopping Street office and Wagakki BAR Ryugu are for yokai participants only.
Q. Can I join as a yokai?
Yes, with advance registration (details). Registration closes when the 250 spots are filled.
